The fundamentals

Four ideas behind every estimate.

From the rating plate to the final bill, energy use can be understood in a simple sequence.

STEP 01 W

Power Usage

Rated capacity measured in Watts (W) or kilowatts. It denotes instantaneous electrical demand when an appliance is operational.

Unit scale: 50 W TV to 2,400 W kettle
STEP 02 T

Hours of Use

The active operating cycle combined with passive standby time per day. Continuous items run in cycled bursts.

Active cycle vs phantom draw
STEP 03 kW

Consumption

Standardised as kilowatt-hours (kWh). This measures power consumed across a specific duration over time.

Metric: 1 kWh = 1,000 watt-hours
STEP 04 $

Running Cost

Calculated against regional energy tariffs. Australian domestic tariffs often sit around 30 to 35 cents per kWh.

Formula: kWh × tariff rate ($AUD)

National grid context

Why does energy consumption matter?

In typical Australian homes, heating and cooling represent a large share of baseline energy use, with water heating, refrigeration, cooking and entertainment making up much of the remaining demand. Understanding wattage profiles enables informed purchasing and targeted reduction.

Reference notes

Frequently Asked Questions

Common questions about appliance ratings and household electricity use.

Rated wattage, daily operating hours, temperature settings, maintenance, appliance age and energy-rating performance all influence actual consumption.
Electricity is recorded in kilowatt-hours (kWh). One kilowatt-hour equals 1,000 watts of electrical power used continuously for one hour.
Standby power is electricity drawn while equipment appears to be switched off or sleeping. A power-board switch can help isolate devices that are not being used.
